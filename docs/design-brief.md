# Design brief

**Owner: Natasha.** This is the creative direction for nycbalboa.com. Agents follow it for every visual and copy change. Natasha updates it whenever her vision changes — just ask the agent to "update the design brief."

## Who the site is for

- **Total beginners** who saw a flyer or Instagram post and want to try Balboa — make the first step obvious and unintimidating.
- **Visiting dancers** in NYC for a few days — show what's happening this week fast.
- **Local dancers** — a reliable hub for where to dance, learn, and practice.

Most visitors are on a phone, often on the way to (or at) a dance.

## Feel

Vintage 1930s–40s swing, made warm and welcoming. Classic, readable, a little elegant — like a well-designed dance hall program. Not trendy, not neon, not corporate.

## Voice

Friendly, inclusive, and concise. Talk like an organizer welcoming someone at the door. "No partner or experience necessary." Avoid jargon a beginner wouldn't know, or explain it.

## Current visual system

| Element | Current choice |
|---|---|
| Headings font | Newsreader (serif) |
| Body font | DM Sans |
| Text | Ink `#211b1a` |
| Background | Warm paper `#f4efe5`, cards `#fffdf8` |
| Main accent | Burgundy `#832b42` (dark: `#5c1d2e`) |
| Secondary accent | Brass `#bd9145` |
| Supporting | Sage `#647469`, muted text `#746b63`, borders `#ddd1c1` |

These live as variables at the top of `src/layouts/Layout.astro`. Changing a variable changes it everywhere.

## Principles

- Beginners first: the path to "how do I start?" should be one tap from the homepage.
- Fewer, clearer things beat more things.
- Every page must look good on a phone.
- Photos and visuals should feel real and human — actual NYC dancers over stock imagery.
